# 3D — ORBITAL

The scene exists for one reason: to give the delay a body. There is no lighting
rig, no environment map, no texture, and no model. Every visible thing is a
primitive, a point cloud, or a shader running on one of those.

## 1. The spine

The ladder is a straight line along **z**.

* `SPINE_GAP = 640` scene units between rung origins.
* Rung *n* sits at `position = [0, 0, -640 * n]`, so the Moon is at the origin and
  First Light is 4,480 units away.
* The camera's z is `-cameraPosition * SPINE_GAP`, plus the rung's authored offset.
* The thread (section 6) spans `RUNGS.length * SPINE_GAP + 800 = 5,920` units,
  starting 400 units in front of the Moon, so the camera is always inside it.

Staging is compressed, deliberately and visibly: the real ratios between these
objects are 10²⁶ apart, and no camera can show that. The compression is admitted on
the page (*"Distances here are compressed so they can be seen. The numbers are
not."*), and the *numbers* — the look-back times — are never touched.

## 2. Camera

`FRAME = { near: 0.5, far: 7000, fov: 43 }`. FOV 43 is a mild telephoto: it flattens
the objects a little, which is how a telescope actually presents them, and it keeps
the wide-corona shots (Sun) from distorting.

Frames are authored per rung and interpolated linearly; there is no spline and no
hand-tuning at runtime. `frameAt(pos, portrait)` in `scene/CameraRig.tsx` is the
only place a camera position is computed.

| Rung | `pos` | `look` |
|------|-------|--------|
| Opening frame | `[0, 18, 330]` | `[0, 0, 0]` |
| 01 Moon | `[2, 15, 190]` | `[0, 0, 0]` |
| 02 Sun | `[40, 46, 470]` | `[0, 0, 0]` |
| 03 Voyager 1 | `[30, 9, 58]` | `[0, 0, 0]` |
| 04 Betelgeuse | `[-26, 34, 430]` | `[0, 0, 0]` |
| 05 Crab | `[0, 18, 330]` | `[0, -4, 0]` |
| 06 Galactic Centre | `[0, 14, 400]` | `[0, -24, 0]` |
| 07 Andromeda | `[0, 170, 720]` | `[0, 0, 0]` |
| 08 First Light | `[0, 6, 190]` | `[0, 40, -420]` |

Between rungs the position and look vectors are lerped, and the z term is the
camera's rung position — so the camera *travels* rather than cross-fading. The
opening frame is a lerp from `HERO` to the Moon frame across `pos` 0 → -0.55.

**Damping.** `live.camera = damp(live.camera, live.target, 5.5, dt)`, with `dt`
clamped to 50ms. A tab that was hidden for a second resumes without a jump.

**Portrait bias.** When `size.height > size.width`, the frame is adjusted:
`x *= 0.45`, `y = y * 1.05 + 6`, `z *= 1.38`. The subject moves up and back so the
plate (which becomes a full-width card on phones) never covers it.

**Pointer parallax.** On fine pointers, the camera is offset by up to `min(16,
distance * 0.06)` units toward the cursor, smoothed at `3.2/s`; off for coarse
pointers and reduced motion. It is small on purpose: enough that the scene feels
held, not enough to detach the object from its plate.

## 3. Sleep bands

`useRung(i)` marks a group visible only when `|live.camera - i| < 1.15` — the rung
the camera is inside, plus the one it is travelling to. Everything else is skipped
whole (no matrices, no draw calls, no per-object `useFrame` work beyond the test).

This is the single biggest reason eight environments fit in one frame budget. It is
also why the draw-call counts in section 7 are small: they are measured for one
rung at a time.

## 4. What is in each rung

| Rung | Objects | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| 01 Moon | Sphere r=60 (72×36), Earth sphere r=11 at `[-96, 46, -40]`, Earth glow plane 92, pulse marker | Two `createSurfaceMaterial` instances: rock (crater 0.8, scale 0.013) and Earth (crater 0.2, scale 0.06). The Moon rotates at 0.014 rad/s |
| 02 Sun | Sphere r=150 (96×48), two corona planes (720 and 1500), photon marker | `hot: 1` surface; corona intensities are driven by the scrub; the photon lerps from the surface to the camera across the last 30% of the dial |
| 03 Voyager 1 | Dish (partial sphere r=21, θ 0.8π), bus box 12×9×9, boom cylinder 34, RTG cylinder 11×4.4, mast cylinder 26, signal ring, pulse marker | Schematic, not a portrait. Pulse is `oneWay` |
| 04 Betelgeuse | Sphere r=120 (80×40), halo plane 620, shockwave ring | `hot: 1`; a breathing scale of ±1.8% at 0.42 rad/s; the ring is a `ringGeometry(36, 46)` scaled 0.6 → 4.0 over 4.2s |
| 05 Crab | Filament point cloud (tier count), guest-star glow plane 420, pulsar core sphere r=3.2, two cone beams (r=22, h=240) | The 20% spherical core plus a 42–150 radius shell, squashed to 0.62; beams counter-rotate at 0.5 → 1.6 rad/s depending on mode |
| 06 Galactic Centre | Dust cloud point cloud (tier count), dust band plane 1100×320, photon ring, black-hole shadow circle r=33 | The band is a procedural fbm alpha; the three modes damp `uOpacity` between 0.94 and 0.12 and fade in the 1.3mm photon ring |
| 07 Andromeda | Galaxy point cloud (tier count), spin group, arriving wavefront ring | 20% bulge (r ≤ 120, squashed 0.6) + 80% two-arm disc (r ≤ 430, log-spiral `theta = r * 0.0115 + arm ± 0.6`); the group rotates at 0.02 rad/s |

## 5. Materials

Seven factories in `scene/shaders/materials.ts`, all `ShaderMaterial`, no textures:

| Factory | Used by | Key uniforms |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| `createSurfaceMaterial` | Moon, Earth, Sun, Betelgeuse, Voyager body/dish | `uColorA/B`, `uHot`, `uScale`, `uCrater`, `uDrift`, `uOpacity`, `uLightDir` |
| `createPointsMaterial` | Starfield, thread motes, Crab filaments, Core cloud, Andromeda arms, First Light motes | `uColorA/B`, `uSize`, `uTwinkle`, `uOpacity`, `uPixelRatio` |
| `createRibbonMaterial` | The thread | `uColor`, `uCamera`, `uTravel` |
| `createShellMaterial` | First Light | `uColorA/B`, `uTemp`, `uOpacity` |
| `createGlowMaterial` | Coronae, halos, Earth glow, shockwave, photon ring, guest star | `uColor`, `uIntensity`, `uCore` |
| `createBeamMaterial` | Crab pulsar beams | `uColor` |
| `createDustMaterial` | Galactic Centre band | `uColor`, `uOpacity` |

All of them read `shared.uTime` **by reference** (`src/scene/shared.ts`), plus
`shared.uCamera` and `shared.uTravel` where relevant. One write per frame reaches
every shader in the scene.

Shared GLSL (`shaders/glsl.ts`): a hash, a 3D value-noise `noise3`, a 5-octave
`fbm` and a `ridged` variant. Surfaces use `fbm` for colour and `ridged` for
craters; the Crab guest star, the First Light shell and the Core dust band all use
the same kit with different scales, so nothing looks like it came from a different
project.

**Lighting is faked, per material, on purpose.** There is no `DirectionalLight` and
no `MeshStandardMaterial`: a Lambert term against `uLightDir` with a floor of 0.045
gives a hard terminator and near-black shadow, and `uHot` mixes in an emissive term
with limb darkening. Materials do not receive shadows, because nothing in this scene
casts one.

### The one shader bug worth recording

The first build used `modelMatrix` in the fragment stage of `FRAG_SURFACE` to build
a view vector. `modelMatrix` is injected into the *vertex* prefix only, so the
shader failed to link and three logged `THREE.WebGLProgram: Shader Error` /
`useProgram` warnings; the surfaces rendered black. The fix is a `vWorld` varying:
the vertex stage multiplies by `modelMatrix` and passes the world position down, and
the fragment stage computes `viewDir = normalize(cameraPosition - vWorld)`. After
the fix every program links with no `THREE.WebGLProgram` or `useProgram` warning in
the console; the production build compiles four programs on a fresh load at tier
`high` and eight once all eight rungs have drawn at least once (several materials
only compile the first time something needs them; the dev server, where Fast
Refresh re-creates a few, reports six on a fresh load and eight at the end).

One warning does reach the console in both dev and production, and it is not ours:
the current `@react-three/fiber` (9.8.1, the latest) still constructs a
`THREE.Clock` internally, and three 0.186 prints `THREE.Clock: This module has
been deprecated. Please use THREE.Timer instead.` once per page load. There is no
cleaner fix available at the pinned versions than waiting for the upstream
migration; the renderer itself logs nothing.

## 6. Sky and thread

* **Starfield** — a point cloud on a sphere of radius 2400–2920, *carried with the
  camera* (`position.copy(camera.position)`) so it never runs out and never needs a
  skydome. 45% of its stars are pulled into a band around the equator: the galaxy we
  are inside of, seen from within. Count by tier: 6000 / 3000 / 1500.
* **Thread** — one long ribbon plane (54 × 5,920) plus a mote cloud. It is the only
  object present at every rung, and it is what makes the compression honest: the
  visitor is travelling along a single photon's route, not cutting between
  photographs. Its alpha fades in between 2 and 120 units of distance from the
  camera and out between 1500 and 3000; `uTravel` (0 = holding, 1 = mid-flight)
  raises its intensity and speeds the motes from 50 to 950 units/s.

## 7. Cost, measured

Draw calls and triangles for the frame the camera holds on each rung, read from the
`?hud=1` hook (`renderer.info.render`) at 1440×900, tier `high`. The recipe is
exact, because the number depends on it: set `live.camera` **and** `live.target` to
the rung index, wait four rendered frames, then read. A camera caught mid-damp
reports a smaller frame, so the same rung can read 7 or 9 depending on when you
look — the table below is the hold, which is what the visitor actually sits inside:

| Rung | Draw calls | Triangles |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| opening frame (hero) | 6 | 6,564 |
| 01 Moon | 9 | 15,592 |
| 02 Sun | 15 | 17,696 |
| 03 Voyager 1 | 14 | 17,376 |
| 04 Betelgeuse | 16 | 8,918 |
| 05 Crab | 13 | 6,880 |
| 06 Galactic Centre | 12 | 638 |
| 07 Andromeda | 9 | 2,276 |
| 08 First Light | 6 | 2,210 |

The count includes the neighbouring rung inside the ±1.15 wake band, which is the
honest number: while the camera holds on the Sun, the Moon has not been switched
off yet and the Sun's own arrival is being drawn. Point clouds dominate the
geometry: a 14,000-star galaxy is one draw call and zero triangles. The peaks are
the sphere segment counts (the Sun is 96×48) and the ring geometries. Nothing here
needs an LOD system; the sleep bands do the work.

Sixteen calls at the heaviest hold, on a page that contains eight environments, a
full-length thread and a star field.

`?hud=1` also exposes the renderer to the console as `window.__orbital` — see
docs/PERFORMANCE.md for the measurement recipe.

## 8. Tier table

`src/lib/quality.ts` — one table, three devices. The governor can move a tier at
runtime; the scene reads counts from it, so a change is a re-mesh of the point
clouds, not a rebuild of the page.

| Tier | DPR | stars | motes | galaxy | filaments | cloud | ribbon |
|------|-----|-------|-------|--------|-----------|-------|--------|
| high | 2 | 6000 | 900 | 14000 | 5200 | 9000 | yes |
| mid | 1.5 | 3000 | 420 | 7000 | 2600 | 4500 | yes |
| low | 1.25 | 1500 | 200 | 3200 | 1200 | 2000 | no |

`low` also turns antialiasing off (`gl={{ antialias: tier !== 'low' }}`). The story
is identical at every tier: no rung is missing, no copy is hidden, no mechanism is
disabled. Only density changes.
